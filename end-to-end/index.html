Charles Elena

One studio, first sketch to final pack-down.

We design and build the things people walk up to on a show floor: the stand itself, the screens on it, the AI demo holding the conversation, the robot working the crowd, and the content playing on the walls.

Concept, fabrication, software, freight and install all sit with one team. Nothing is briefed out to a second agency and nothing gets lost in the gap between them — which is why the five things that follow are one offer, not five.

01 — Conceptual design

Before anything is built, it is argued.

Every experience starts as a question about what a visitor should feel. We work it out in the open: wireframes, look-and-feel routes, competing concepts, a chosen direction. Clients see the thinking, not just the result.

02 — Booth design and build

Drawn, engineered, freighted, standing.

Structure, graphics, lighting, LED, install and strike. We have built on the floor at Mobile World Congress twice, for SAP at Sapphire, for TM Forum and for Mastercard.

03 — AI demo experience

The reason people stop walking.

Interactive AI demos built for a noisy hall — touch, screen and voice, running reliably for eight hours a day. Open one.

04 — Robotics

A robot on a stand is a logistics problem wearing a costume.

We source, programme and choreograph the robot, then handle the part nobody enjoys: freight, carnet, customs, on-site commissioning, a rehearsal before doors, and a technician who stays for the show. It arrives, it works, it goes home.

05 — Motion and panel videos

Content cut for the wall it lives on.

Side-panel loops from Mobile World Congress, mastered to the exact pixel dimensions of the booth hardware. Each portal gets its own narrative, timed to run all day without an edit point a passer-by can catch.
